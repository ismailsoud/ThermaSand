How It Works

Turning electricity into heat, storing it in sand, and delivering it on demand.

  • Charge: Excess grid or solar electricity powers a central resistive heater.
  • Store: 5L of sand (prototype scale) is insulated with high-density Rock Wool, holding heat for hours.
  • Discharge: A blower fan forces air through a copper serpentine coil submerged in the hot sand, providing hot air output.

The Economic Edge: Arbitrage in the Moroccan Grid

Morocco’s Time-of-Use tariff structure is the primary "fuel" behind ThermaSand’s ROI.

  • The "Heure de Pointe" factor: Moroccan factories and hotels face heavy surcharges during peak evening hours.
  • The AI advantage: ThermaSand identifies "Heure Creuse" (often 11 PM to 7 AM) to charge the sand at the lowest rates.
  • Cost displacement: Discharging stored heat during the day avoids expensive peak-hour electricity or imported butane/propane.

Industry-Specific Benefits: Morocco’s Core Sectors

Agri-Food & Textiles

Factories in Agadir (canning) or Casablanca (textiles) need high volumes of process heat for drying and sterilization.

  • Fossil fuel replacement: Switch from Fuel Oil N°2 or gas to an electric thermal battery powered by Morocco’s growing wind and solar share.
  • Frugal maintenance: Mechanical systems (blowers, coils) are serviceable by Moroccan industrial teams.

Hospitality Sector

Hotels and resorts require massive hot water volumes for showers, kitchens, and heated pools.

  • Lithium vs. sand: Sand is inert and fireproof, avoiding thermal runaway risks in basements.
  • Thermal heart: A large sand vault provides safe, centralized hot water storage.

Public Infrastructure

Hospitals and universities require dependable 24/7 heat for sterilization, laundry, and services.

  • Continuous reliability: A charged thermal buffer smooths grid fluctuations and ensures heat availability.
